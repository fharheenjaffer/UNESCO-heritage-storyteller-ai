# India Gate

## Location
New Delhi, Delhi, India

## UNESCO Year
null

## Historical Background
- India Gate was designed by British architect Edwin Lutyens and built between 1921 and 1931 as part of the broader construction of New Delhi as the new capital of British India.
- The monument was conceived as a war memorial to honour around 84,000 soldiers of the British Indian Army who died fighting in World War I and the Third Anglo-Afghan War, with their names inscribed on the structure.
- After India's independence, the site took on additional national significance, and in 1972 the Amar Jawan Jyoti (Flame of the Immortal Soldier) was added beneath the arch to honour Indian soldiers who died in the 1971 India-Pakistan war.
- In 2019, this flame was moved and merged with a newer National War Memorial nearby, dedicated more broadly to Indian soldiers killed in conflicts since independence, while India Gate itself continues to honour the earlier colonial-era war dead.

## Architecture
India Gate is a triumphal arch built of red sandstone and granite, standing about 42 metres tall, drawing stylistic inspiration from monuments such as the Arc de Triomphe in Paris while incorporating Mughal and Indian decorative elements typical of Lutyens' broader design for New Delhi. The names of the soldiers it commemorates are inscribed directly onto the stone surface of the structure. It sits at the centre of a large ceremonial boulevard, Rajpath (now Kartavya Path), and forms a key visual anchor within Lutyens' planned layout of the capital.

## Interesting Facts
- The names of around 13,300 additional soldiers and officers who died in the Third Anglo-Afghan War of 1919 are inscribed on the monument alongside World War I casualties.
- India Gate anchors one end of the ceremonial Kartavya Path, along which India's annual Republic Day parade is held.
- It was designed by Edwin Lutyens, the same architect responsible for much of New Delhi's colonial-era planning, including the Rashtrapati Bhavan.
- The original eternal flame beneath the arch, lit in 1972, was relocated to the National War Memorial in 2019 as part of a reorganisation of India's war commemoration sites.

## Story Version
Standing at the eastern end of the grand ceremonial Kartavya Path boulevard in New Delhi, the monumental arch of India Gate rises forty-two metres into the capital sky. Designed by Sir Edwin Lutyens and completed in 1931, this triumphal arch of pale red and yellow sandstone commemorates more than eighty thousand British Indian soldiers who died in the First World War and Third Anglo-Afghan War. Walking toward the massive central portal, you can trace thousands of individual soldiers' names etched into the stone walls in solemn remembrance. Beneath the great arch burns the Amar Jawan Jyoti eternal flame, flanked by an inverted rifle and helmet honoring the nation's fallen soldiers since the 1971 war. In the evening, surrounding lawns and fountains fill with families enjoying the cool breeze, while golden floodlights illuminate the sandstone arch. Standing here, you feel the profound intersection of wartime sacrifice, colonial architectural planning, and modern India's enduring civic memory.
