# Group of Monuments at Hampi

## Location
Hampi, Karnataka, India

## UNESCO Year
1986

## Historical Background
- Hampi was the capital of the Vijayanagara Empire, one of the largest and most powerful Hindu kingdoms in South Indian history, which flourished from the fourteenth to the sixteenth century.
- At its peak in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, the city was one of the largest and wealthiest urban centres in the world, drawing accounts from foreign travellers who described bustling markets and grand royal structures.
- In 1565, a coalition of neighbouring Deccan sultanates decisively defeated the Vijayanagara army at the Battle of Talikota, after which the city was sacked and largely abandoned over the following months.
- The ruins were left largely undisturbed for centuries, preserving an extensive complex of temples, market streets, royal enclosures, and irrigation works across a boulder-strewn landscape.

## Architecture
Hampi's monuments span temple architecture, royal and civic structures, and market streets built mainly of granite, reflecting the mature Vijayanagara architectural style, itself a synthesis of earlier Chalukya, Hoysala, Pandya, and Chola influences. The Virupaksha Temple, still active for worship, features a tall gopuram (gateway tower) and an ornately carved mandapa. The Vittala Temple complex is known for its stone chariot and musical pillars that produce different tones when struck. Elsewhere, structures like the Lotus Mahal and elephant stables show Indo-Islamic influence, reflecting the empire's interactions with neighbouring sultanates.

## Interesting Facts
- The site spans roughly 4,100 hectares and contains hundreds of individual monuments spread across a dramatic boulder landscape.
- The stone chariot at the Vittala Temple is such an iconic image that it appears on the Indian fifty-rupee note.
- Some pillars in the Vittala Temple produce musical tones when tapped, earning them the name 'musical pillars'.
- At its height, foreign travellers compared Hampi's markets favourably to major cities of contemporary Europe and Persia in terms of wealth and scale.

## Story Version
Standing among the surreal granite boulder hills of Hampi along the Tungabhadra River, you look out across the vast ruined capital of the Vijayanagara Empire. Founded in the fourteenth century and flourishing under rulers like Krishnadevaraya, this metropolis commanded global maritime trade before being sacked and abandoned in 1565. As you walk down the ancient chariot streets toward the Virupaksha Temple, which has remained an active place of worship through the centuries, the sheer scale of the city unfolds. Wandering through the Royal Centre, you explore the tiered Mahanavami Dibba platform, the monumental Elephant Stables, and the elegant arches of the Lotus Mahal. At the riverside Vittala Temple, you stand in awe before the celebrated stone chariot, surrounded by pillared halls engineered to produce musical tones when struck. The golden evening sun lights up miles of tumbled granite fortifications, aqueducts, and sacred pavilions, transporting your imagination back to when European and Persian chroniclers described Hampi as one of the richest and most magnificent cities in the medieval world.
