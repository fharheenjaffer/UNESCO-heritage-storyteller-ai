# Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CSMT)

## Location
Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

## UNESCO Year
2004

## Historical Background
- The terminus was built by the Great Indian Peninsula Railway between 1878 and 1888, designed by British architect Frederick William Stevens, and originally named Victoria Terminus after Queen Victoria.
- It was constructed to serve as the flagship terminal station of colonial-era railway expansion in western India, symbolising both engineering ambition and imperial prestige.
- The station was renamed Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus in 1996, and later Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus in 2017, honouring the seventeenth-century Maratha ruler Shivaji.
- The station remains a fully functioning major railway hub today, handling large volumes of daily commuter and long-distance traffic while retaining its historic structure.

## Architecture
The building is designed in a High Victorian Gothic Revival style, blending elements of traditional Indian architecture with British Gothic design, a hybrid style sometimes called Indo-Saracenic. It features pointed arches, turrets, a prominent central dome, and elaborate stone carvings depicting flora, fauna, and figures including a statue atop the dome symbolising 'Progress'. The interior showcases ornate ironwork, stained glass, and decorative tile work typical of high Victorian public architecture, unusually applied here at a scale and detail rare even in Britain itself.

## Interesting Facts
- It remains one of the busiest railway stations in India despite being over 130 years old.
- The building blends British Gothic Revival architecture with Indian traditional design elements, a hybrid style found in several colonial-era Indian buildings.
- It was renamed twice after independence, most recently in 2017, to honour Maratha ruler Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj.
- It is one of the few working railway stations in the world with UNESCO World Heritage status.

## Story Version
Stepping outside into the bustling streets of Mumbai, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus commands your attention with its breathtaking Victorian Gothic Revival grandeur. Completed in 1888 to mark Queen Victoria's Golden Jubilee and designed by British architect F.W. Stevens, this monumental terminus merged European Gothic pointed arches and turrets with traditional Indian architectural craft. Walking beneath the grand central dome crowned by a statue of Progress, you enter a soaring passenger concourse filled with vibrant morning light streaming through stained-glass windows. Intricately carved stone brackets depicting Indian flora and fauna adorn the walls, while polished marble columns and decorative tile work highlight the extraordinary craftsmanship. Today, as one of the world's few working railway terminals recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, millions of daily commuters rush across its historic stone platforms. Standing amidst the lively movement of Mumbai, you can feel the pulse of a living landmark that has linked rail travel, imperial history, and local urban life for over a century.
