# Charminar

## Location
Hyderabad, Telangana, India

## UNESCO Year
null

## Historical Background
- Charminar was built in 1591 by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, the fifth ruler of the Qutb Shahi dynasty, to mark the founding of the city of Hyderabad after he moved his capital from nearby Golconda Fort.
- According to popular tradition, the monument was built to commemorate the end of a plague epidemic that had struck the region, with the ruler vowing to build a mosque and monument at the spot where he prayed for the city's deliverance.
- The structure was positioned at the intersection of the city's two main thoroughfares, intentionally designed as the symbolic and geographic centre of the newly planned city of Hyderabad.
- Over the following centuries the surrounding area grew into a dense commercial district, and Charminar became closely associated with the identity of Hyderabad's old city, an association it retains today.

## Architecture
Charminar is built primarily of granite and lime mortar in an Indo-Islamic style with Persian influences, featuring four grand arches facing the cardinal directions that support four minarets, each rising about 56 metres and consisting of four storeys with a balcony ring. A mosque occupies the upper floor of the structure, reportedly among the oldest mosques in Hyderabad. The building's name, meaning 'Four Minarets' or 'Four Towers', reflects its most distinctive architectural feature, and its symmetrical, open design was intended to allow the structure to serve as both a landmark and a functioning place of worship and public gathering.

## Interesting Facts
- Charminar's four minarets each have a spiral staircase of 149 steps leading to the top.
- It sits at the centre of a bustling market area still known for pearl and bangle trading, a tradition dating back centuries.
- The monument's design has made it an emblem of Hyderabad, appearing widely in the city's branding and popular imagery.
- Legend holds that a secret tunnel once connected Charminar to Golconda Fort, though no confirmed archaeological evidence of such a tunnel has been found.

## Story Version
Standing at the bustling intersection of Old Hyderabad, the iconic four-arched silhouette of Charminar rises in dignified Indo-Islamic elegance. Commissioned in 1591 by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, the fifth ruler of the Qutb Shahi dynasty, this monumental landmark was constructed to commemorate the end of a devastating plague and mark the founding of Hyderabad. Gazing up at its four grand granite arches opening to the cardinal directions, your eyes follow the four soaring minarets rising fifty-six metres into the sky, each encircled by graceful balconies. On the upper level rests one of Hyderabad's oldest mosques, overlooking centuries of urban history. Below, the surrounding streets of Laad Bazaar buzz with activity, where craftsmen have traded vibrant bangles and pearls since royal times. While local folklore whispers of ancient secret escape tunnels once connecting Charminar to Golconda Fort, the true magic lies in experiencing this timeless gateway that remains the proud cultural and architectural heart of Hyderabad.
