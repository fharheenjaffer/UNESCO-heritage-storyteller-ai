# Humayun's Tomb

## Location
Nizamuddin East, Delhi, India

## UNESCO Year
1993

## Historical Background
- Humayun's Tomb was commissioned around 1565 by Empress Bega Begum, the widow of Mughal emperor Humayun, roughly nine years after his death, and was completed in 1572.
- It is considered the first grand dynastic mausoleum built by the Mughals in India and set a precedent that architects would later draw on for later monuments, including the Taj Mahal.
- The tomb complex became a refuge and later a prison for the last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, who took shelter there during the 1857 uprising before being captured by British forces.
- The site fell into disrepair over subsequent centuries until major conservation efforts, particularly since the late twentieth century, restored its gardens and structures to something close to their original form.

## Architecture
The tomb is built of red sandstone with white and black marble detailing, set at the centre of a formal charbagh garden divided by water channels into four quadrants, in the Persian garden tradition. It sits on a high, arched sandstone platform and is crowned by a double dome, an architectural device that would later be used at the Taj Mahal. The building's symmetry, use of chhatris (domed pavilions), and combination of Persian and Indian design elements mark an important transition point in Mughal architecture.

## Interesting Facts
- Architectural historians regard it as an important design precursor to the Taj Mahal, built roughly eighty years earlier.
- The complex holds the graves of more than 150 Mughal family members, earning it a reputation as a 'dormitory of the Mughals'.
- It was among the first Mughal structures in India to use a true double dome.
- The last Mughal emperor was captured by the British near this tomb in 1857, marking a symbolic end to Mughal rule.

## Story Version
Passing through the grand southern gateway into the tranquil gardens of Nizamuddin East, Humayun's Tomb rises before you with sublime geometric harmony. Commissioned around 1565 by Empress Bega Begum in memory of her husband emperor Humayun, this majestic sanctuary stands as the first grand dynastic garden-mausoleum of the Mughal dynasty. You stroll along raised stone walkways through the formal Persian-style charbagh garden, where bubbling water channels divide the grounds into four symmetrical quadrants representing paradise. Climbing the arched sandstone plinth, you can admire the refined contrast of red sandstone inlaid with white and black marble detailing beneath a magnificent white double dome, flanked by graceful domed chhatris. Inside the central chamber, soft filtered light illuminates the historic resting place of Humayun, surrounded by the graves of over one hundred and fifty Mughal royals. Standing on the wide upper terrace, you can sense the architectural brilliance that eighty years later would inspire the Taj Mahal, while remembering that the last Mughal emperor found brief refuge here in 1857.
