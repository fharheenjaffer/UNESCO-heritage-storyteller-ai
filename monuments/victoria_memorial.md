# Victoria Memorial

## Location
Kolkata, West Bengal, India

## UNESCO Year
null

## Historical Background
- The Victoria Memorial was conceived by Lord Curzon, then Viceroy of India, following the death of Queen Victoria in 1901, as a grand monument to commemorate her reign and, more broadly, British imperial rule in India.
- Construction began in 1906 and took roughly two decades, funded substantially through public donations from both British officials and Indian princely states, reflecting the scale of imperial patronage the project attracted.
- The building was formally opened in 1921 by the Prince of Wales, having been designed by architect William Emerson in a style intended to rival great European memorial buildings.
- After India's independence, the building was repurposed as a museum documenting the history of Kolkata (then Calcutta) and colonial India, rather than being retained purely as a monument to British monarchy.

## Architecture
The Victoria Memorial is built of white Makrana marble, the same marble used in the Taj Mahal, and combines British and Mughal architectural elements in a style sometimes described as Indo-Saracenic. The building features a large central dome, flanked by smaller domed corner towers, extensive galleries, and a bronze angel sculpture atop the main dome. It is set within expansive formal gardens designed by landscape architect David Prain, giving the monument a park-like setting distinct from more built-up colonial-era structures elsewhere in Kolkata.

## Interesting Facts
- The building today functions as a museum housing an extensive collection of colonial-era paintings, sculptures, and historical artefacts related to the British period in India.
- The bronze weather-vane style angel atop its main dome is designed to rotate with the wind.
- It was funded substantially by voluntary contributions, including significant donations from Indian princely states, alongside British government and public funding.
- The surrounding gardens span over 60 acres, making the memorial one of the largest green public spaces in central Kolkata.

## Story Version
Strolling through sixty-four acres of landscaped gardens in Kolkata, the radiant white marble dome of the Victoria Memorial rises gracefully beside the Maidan. Commissioned by Viceroy Lord Curzon following the death of Queen Victoria in 1901 and completed in 1921, this majestic monument was designed by William Emerson in a grand Indo-Saracenic style blending British classical architecture with Mughal domes and Rajasthani chhatris. Approaching the wide stone terrace, you discover that the exterior is built of white Makrana marble sourced from the exact same quarries in Rajasthan that supplied the Taj Mahal. High atop the central fifty-five-metre dome, a bronze figure of the Angel of Victory rotates with the coastal wind. Inside, cool marble galleries house historic oil paintings, royal artifacts, and colonial records chronicling the history of Calcutta as the capital of British India. Reflected in the surrounding ornamental water pools, the memorial offers visitors an evocative journey through India's colonial architectural past.
