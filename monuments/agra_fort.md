# Agra Fort (Red Fort of Agra)

## Location
Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India

## UNESCO Year
1983

## Historical Background
- A fort has stood on this site since at least the eleventh century, but the structure visible today was largely rebuilt in red sandstone by the Mughal emperor Akbar starting in 1565, on the ruins of an earlier fortification held by the Lodi dynasty.
- Successive Mughal emperors, notably Shah Jahan, expanded the fort and added white marble palaces and mosques within its walls, shifting the aesthetic from Akbar's austere sandstone style toward more ornate Mughal architecture.
- The fort served as the primary residence of the Mughal emperors until 1638, when Shah Jahan moved the capital to Delhi. It later became the site of Shah Jahan's house arrest by his son Aurangzeb in his final years.
- During the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the fort changed hands several times among Marathas, Jats, and eventually the British East India Company, which used parts of it for military purposes.

## Architecture
Agra Fort is a massive walled complex nearly 2.5 kilometres in circumference, enclosed by double ramparts of red sandstone rising about 20 metres, with a moat originally filled by the Yamuna River. Within the walls, the architectural style shifts from Akbar's robust Hindu-influenced sandstone buildings to the delicate marble pavilions, such as the Khas Mahal and Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace), added later by Shah Jahan. The fort combines defensive military architecture with residential palaces, audience halls, and mosques.

## Interesting Facts
- Only about 25 percent of the fort's interior structures are open to the public today; much of it remains under military control.
- Shah Jahan spent his final years imprisoned in the fort's Musamman Burj tower, from which he could see the Taj Mahal in the distance.
- The fort contains the Pearl Mosque (Moti Masjid), built entirely of white marble.
- It was the first Indian monument inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, alongside the Taj Mahal, Ajanta Caves, and Ellora Caves in 1983.

## Story Version
Approaching Agra Fort along the Yamuna River, you stand beneath colossal red sandstone ramparts that rise twenty metres high and encircle nearly two and a half kilometres of history. Entering through the massive gateway, you step into the fortified world that Mughal emperor Akbar began rebuilding in 1565 upon older medieval ruins. As you walk deeper into the citadel, the rugged sandstone walls of defensive bastions give way to the delicate, opulent world fashioned by his grandson Shah Jahan. Passing through grand courtyards, you reach the luminous white marble pavilions of the Khas Mahal, the glistening Sheesh Mahal Mirror Palace, and the serene Pearl Mosque. The gentle breeze carries echoes of royal durbars and military campaigns from when this citadel served as the heart of the Mughal empire. Standing at the high Musamman Burj tower, your gaze drifts across the river toward the distant Taj Mahal. Here, Shah Jahan spent his final years under house arrest by his son Aurangzeb, quietly watching the marble monument across the water until his death.
