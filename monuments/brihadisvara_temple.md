# Brihadisvara Temple, Thanjavur (Great Living Chola Temples)

## Location
Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu, India

## UNESCO Year
1987

## Historical Background
- The Brihadisvara Temple was built by the Chola emperor Rajaraja I and completed around 1010 CE, at the height of Chola imperial power in South India.
- The temple was conceived as a statement of Chola political and religious authority, dedicated to Shiva, and was richly endowed with land, gold, and resources drawn from the empire's extensive trade and conquest.
- Inscriptions carved into the temple walls provide detailed administrative records of the period, including donations, temple staff, and even details of dancers and musicians employed at the site, making it a valuable historical archive as well as a monument.
- The temple has remained in continuous use as an active place of worship for over a thousand years, distinguishing it from many other ancient monuments that fell out of religious use.

## Architecture
The temple is built of granite in the Dravidian architectural style, dominated by a vimana (temple tower) rising about 66 metres, among the tallest of its kind for its era, capped by a single monolithic dome-shaped capstone weighing an estimated 80 tonnes. The temple complex includes a large enclosed courtyard, a monumental Nandi (bull) statue, and extensive sculptural and painted decoration, some of the original Chola-period murals surviving beneath later layers of paint discovered during restoration work.

## Interesting Facts
- The temple's capstone alone is estimated to weigh around 80 tonnes, believed to have been hauled into place using a long earthen ramp.
- It has functioned as an active temple continuously for over a thousand years.
- Detailed stone inscriptions at the site record administrative and cultural details of Chola-era temple life, including named performers and craftsmen.
- Restorers later found older Chola-period murals hidden beneath layers of Nayak-era repainting on the temple's interior walls.

## Story Version
Walking across the vast granite courtyard in Thanjavur, the monumental vimana tower of Brihadisvara Temple soars over sixty metres into the Tamil Nadu sky. Commissioned by the great Chola emperor Rajaraja I and completed in 1010 CE, this temple stands as an unparalleled triumph of Dravidian architecture. Walking beneath the towering entry gopurams, you are astonished to learn that this immense structure was constructed entirely of interlocking granite blocks without mortar, in a region with no natural stone quarries. Near the entrance of the pillared hall sits a colossal monolithic Nandi bull carved from a single granite boulder. Looking up toward the apex of the central sanctuary, your eyes rest upon the massive octagonal stone shikhara crowning the tower. Inside the sanctum, an immense stone Shiva linga radiates sacred stillness. Centuries of classical Tamil inscriptions carved into the temple base record royal gifts, dancers, and musicians, bringing the imperial power and devotion of the Chola golden age vividly to life.
