# Gol Gumbaz

## Location
Vijayapura (Bijapur), Karnataka, India

## UNESCO Year
null

## Historical Background
- Gol Gumbaz was built as the mausoleum of Sultan Mohammed Adil Shah, the seventh ruler of the Adil Shahi dynasty of the Bijapur Sultanate, with construction beginning around 1626 and continuing until his death in 1656, when work on the surrounding complex stopped.
- The structure was designed by an architect known as Yaqut of Dabul, and was reportedly commissioned to surpass the grandeur of the nearby Ibrahim Rauza, the tomb of the sultan's father, Ibrahim Adil Shah II.
- As the Adil Shahi dynasty's power declined in the following decades, Bijapur eventually fell to Mughal conquest in 1686, after which the city's political importance faded, though the mausoleum itself was left largely intact.
- The monument has since become one of the most recognisable symbols of Deccan Islamic architecture and a defining landmark of present-day Vijayapura.

## Architecture
Gol Gumbaz consists of a massive cube-shaped chamber roughly 47 metres on each side, capped by one of the largest masonry domes in the world, with an internal diameter of about 44 metres, built without any central supporting pillars. The dome's immense weight is transferred to the walls through a system of interlocking arches and pendentives. Around the interior base of the dome runs a circular gallery known as the whispering gallery, where sound is carried and echoed across the space due to the dome's curved acoustics. Four seven-storey octagonal towers stand at each corner of the building, each containing a staircase leading up to the gallery.

## Interesting Facts
- Gol Gumbaz's dome, at about 44 metres across, ranks among the largest pre-modern domes in the world, comparable in scale to St. Peter's Basilica.
- In the whispering gallery, a soft sound made at one point can reportedly be heard clearly on the opposite side, roughly 40 metres away, and can echo multiple times.
- The main chamber below the dome covers about 1,700 square metres, making it one of the largest single-room interior spaces built anywhere before the modern era.
- The mausoleum holds the graves of the sultan along with several family members, all located in a basement crypt beneath the main hall.

## Story Version
Standing before the colossal cubic tomb of Gol Gumbaz in Vijayapura, Karnataka, you are dwarfed by one of the largest masonry domes in the world. Built between 1626 and 1656 as the final resting place of Sultan Mohammed Adil Shah of the Bijapur Sultanate, this Deccan Islamic marvel spans an astonishing forty-four metres across without a single central supporting column. Entering the immense main hall, which covers roughly seventeen hundred square metres of uninterrupted floor space, the scale feels overwhelming. You climb the spiral staircase inside one of the seven-storey corner towers to reach the famous interior Whispering Gallery encircling the base of the dome. Standing on the narrow gallery ledge, you speak a soft whisper toward the curved wall; to your amazement, your voice echoes repeatedly and carries with crystal clarity to someone listening forty metres away on the opposite side. Overlooking the peaceful surrounding gardens, Gol Gumbaz stands as an unforgettable triumph of acoustic engineering and architectural audacity.
