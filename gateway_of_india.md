# Gateway of India

## Location
Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

## UNESCO Year
null

## Historical Background
- The Gateway of India was built to commemorate the visit of King George V and Queen Mary to Mumbai (then Bombay) in December 1911, though the monument itself was not completed until 1924, well after the royal visit it marked.
- The structure was designed by architect George Wittet and constructed on the city's waterfront, at Apollo Bunder, a site that had historically served as a symbolic and functional entry point for arrivals into the city by sea.
- During the British colonial period, the Gateway served as a ceremonial entrance for important dignitaries arriving in India, cementing its role as a formal threshold to the country for visiting officials.
- In a symbolically significant turn, the monument became the site from which the last British troops departed India in 1948, following independence, marking a bookend to its original ceremonial purpose.

## Architecture
The Gateway of India is built primarily from yellow basalt and reinforced concrete, designed in the Indo-Saracenic style that blends Hindu, Islamic, and colonial European architectural elements. Its central arch rises about 26 metres, flanked by decorative details drawing on sixteenth-century Gujarati architectural motifs, including intricate latticework typical of Mughal-era Gujarat. The structure stands directly facing the Arabian Sea at Mumbai's harbour front, historically functioning as a grand ceremonial sea-facing entrance to the city.

## Interesting Facts
- The Gateway of India stands directly opposite the historic Taj Mahal Palace Hotel, one of Mumbai's most iconic hospitality landmarks.
- The last British regiment to leave India after independence departed by boat from this very gateway in 1948.
- Its construction took roughly 13 years, considerably longer than initially planned, due to funding and material supply issues.
- The monument remains one of Mumbai's most popular gathering spots, especially in the evenings, and a common starting point for boat trips to nearby Elephanta Island.

## Story Version
Standing at the water's edge of Mumbai harbour, the Gateway of India rises twenty-six metres high, greeting the rolling waves of the Arabian Sea. Designed by Scottish architect George Wittet, this grand basalt arch was planned to commemorate the 1911 royal visit of King George V and Queen Mary, with construction spanning thirteen years until its completion in 1924. Walking through its broad yellow basalt portal, you admire the intricate lattice jali screens and domed pavilions that define its hybrid Indo-Saracenic style, fusing sixteenth-century Gujarati temple architecture with traditional Islamic arches. Looking outward across the water, you can watch passenger ferries departing for nearby Elephanta Island, while behind you stands the historic Taj Mahal Palace Hotel. It was from this very gateway in 1948 that the last British military regiment boarded boats to depart an independent India, making this sea-facing monument a powerful symbol of historic departures, arrivals, and Mumbai's vibrant coastal spirit.
