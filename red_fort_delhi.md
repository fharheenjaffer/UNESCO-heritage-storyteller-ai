# Red Fort (Lal Qila)

## Location
Old Delhi, Delhi, India

## UNESCO Year
2007

## Historical Background
- The Red Fort was built as the fortified palace of Mughal emperor Shah Jahan when he shifted his capital from Agra to the newly founded city of Shahjahanabad, with construction beginning in 1638 and completed around 1648.
- It served as the residence of Mughal emperors for close to two centuries and was the ceremonial and political centre of the empire at its height.
- The fort's status declined after the 1857 uprising against British rule, following which colonial forces looted many of its treasures and converted much of the palace complex into military barracks.
- The fort gained new symbolic importance after Indian independence in 1947; the Prime Minister has raised the national flag and addressed the nation from its ramparts every Independence Day since.

## Architecture
Built primarily from red sandstone, the fort follows an octagonal plan enclosed by high walls stretching about 2.4 kilometres, with two main gates, the Lahori Gate and Delhi Gate. Inside, marble pavilions such as the Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audience) and Diwan-i-Aam (Hall of Public Audience) showcase Mughal architectural refinement, including the famed Naubat Khana entrance and elaborately carved marble screens. The layout reflects a highly planned garden-palace design influenced by Persian and Timurid traditions, adapted to Mughal India.

## Interesting Facts
- The fort once housed the legendary Peacock Throne before it was carried off by the Persian ruler Nadir Shah in 1739.
- Its ramparts are the site of India's annual Independence Day flag-hoisting ceremony.
- The complex originally had a canal known as the Nahr-i-Behisht (Stream of Paradise) running through its royal apartments.
- It was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007, nearly a quarter-century after Agra Fort and the Taj Mahal.

## Story Version
Walking up to the monumental Lahori Gate in Old Delhi, you are dwarfed by massive red sandstone walls that stretch over two kilometres around Shahjahanabad. Commissioned in 1638 by emperor Shah Jahan when he shifted his imperial capital from Agra, the Red Fort was designed as the grand political and ceremonial centre of the Mughal Empire. Passing through the vaulted Naubat Khana entrance, you step into expansive courtyards where the emperor once held court. At the Diwan-i-Aam and Diwan-i-Khas, intricate marble screens and delicate archways reveal where the legendary Peacock Throne once stood before being carried off in 1739. You can trace the path of the Nahr-i-Behisht, the Stream of Paradise canal that once brought cooling waters flowing through the royal apartments. Looking up at the formidable ramparts, history stretches from seventeenth-century imperial splendour to the 1857 uprising, and finally to modern India, where the national flag is raised every Independence Day, connecting centuries of heritage across these historic red stone stones.
