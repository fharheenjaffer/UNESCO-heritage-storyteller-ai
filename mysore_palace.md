# Mysore Palace (Amba Vilas Palace)

## Location
Mysuru, Karnataka, India

## UNESCO Year
null

## Historical Background
- A palace has stood at this site since the fourteenth century under the Wadiyar dynasty, but the structure seen today was rebuilt after a devastating fire destroyed the earlier wooden palace in 1897.
- The present palace was commissioned by the Wadiyar rulers and designed by the British architect Henry Irwin, with construction taking place between 1897 and 1912, funded largely by the royal treasury.
- The Wadiyar dynasty used the palace as their primary royal residence and centre of administration through the early twentieth century, continuing even after India's independence, when many princely states, including Mysore, were integrated into the Indian union.
- Descendants of the Wadiyar family still occupy a private portion of the palace complex, while the main palace has become a major public museum and tourist landmark for the city of Mysuru.

## Architecture
The palace is built in the Indo-Saracenic architectural style, a hybrid approach popular in colonial-era India that blends Hindu, Islamic, Rajput, and Gothic elements. It features domed towers, ornate archways, and richly decorated halls, most notably the Durbar Hall with its gilded ceiling and rows of ornamental columns, and the Kalyana Mandapa (marriage pavilion) with its stained-glass, peacock-themed domed ceiling. The exterior is built primarily of grey granite with pink marble domes, and the palace grounds include gardens, temples, and courtyards reflecting the scale of a former princely capital.

## Interesting Facts
- The palace is illuminated with nearly 100,000 light bulbs every Sunday night and during the annual Dasara festival, drawing large crowds.
- Mysuru's Dasara celebrations, centred on the palace, have been celebrated for centuries and remain one of Karnataka's largest cultural festivals.
- The palace is reported to be one of the most visited monuments in India, drawing more annual visitors than even the Taj Mahal in some tourism surveys.
- The Kalyana Mandapa's stained-glass ceiling depicts peacock motifs and was manufactured in Glasgow, Scotland, reflecting the era's international craftsmanship.

## Story Version
Approaching the sprawling royal grounds of Mysore Palace in Karnataka, you are greeted by an opulent spectacle of Indo-Saracenic royal architecture. Commissioned by the Wadiyar dynasty after an older wooden palace burned in 1897, this grand royal residence was designed by British architect Henry Irwin and completed in 1912. Stepping through the ornate entry halls, you marvel at the seamless blend of Hindu, Muslim, Rajput, and Gothic styles. Inside the private Durbar Hall, ornate cast-iron pillars, stained-glass ceilings imported from Scotland, and richly decorated peacock motifs showcase peerless royal craftsmanship. Walking across glazed tile floors toward the Gombe Thotti doll pavilion, you can see royal palanquins and the famed golden howdah throne used during historic Dasara festival processions. When evening arrives on festive occasions, nearly one hundred thousand electric light bulbs illuminate the palace facade in a glowing golden contour, transforming the royal estate into an enchanting jewel of south Indian princely heritage.
